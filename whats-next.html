What's next? / A personal north star

Designing for a larger world.

I've spent 14 years designing how people, businesses and operational systems come together. What excites me now is exploring how that understanding can shape the systems that power everyday life — at the scale of industries, communities and cities.

The next frontier

From making complex commerce work to making complex environments work better for the people within them.

Not a departure from what I know. An expansion of where I can apply it.

01 / The why

I've always been drawn to what happens beneath the surface.

A customer sees a product arrive at their doorstep. Behind that seemingly simple experience is an intricate network of inventory, people, technology, decisions, incentives and physical infrastructure. Having designed across many parts of that network, I've learned that the quality of an experience is often determined long before someone opens an app.

What fascinates me is how all these moving parts come together. Why certain systems work effortlessly, why others create friction, and how a seemingly small decision in one part of an ecosystem can create disproportionately large effects elsewhere.

Commerce has been my training ground for understanding this complexity. I now find myself asking a larger question: what if the same way of thinking could improve how we move through a city, access services, run businesses, or interact with the institutions that shape our lives?

The most meaningful technology may be the kind we barely notice — because it simply makes life work better.

02 / The territories

Where my curiosity keeps taking me.

These aren't industries I want to collect on a résumé. They're intersecting problem spaces where I see immense potential for human-centred design, systems thinking and technology to make a tangible difference.

01

Cities as interconnected systems

Mobility, urban logistics, public services and the everyday interactions that make a city function. I'm interested in how cities can become more responsive to the people who live, work and move through them — without making those people navigate more complexity.

How might a city feel like a coherent experience rather than a collection of disconnected services?

02

Intelligent, adaptive ecosystems

AI, machine learning and data science as ways to understand changing conditions, anticipate needs and improve decisions. Not intelligence for its own sake, but systems that learn, adapt and support human judgement with appropriate oversight.

How can systems become increasingly self-optimising while remaining understandable, accountable and human?

03

The physical–digital continuum

What happens when we stop treating the screen as the boundary of the experience? I want to explore how digital services can respond to real environments, people, movement, infrastructure and constraints — and how physical experiences can become more intuitive through technology.

What would we design differently if the real world, not the interface, were our starting point?

04

Public–private ecosystems

Interoperable platforms that bring citizens, businesses, governments and service providers together. I'm curious about the role of design in shaping shared workflows, standards, incentives, trust and inclusive access across organisational boundaries.

How can independently operated systems collaborate without passing their complexity on to the people they serve?

05

The future of commerce and movement

Commerce, supply networks, mobility and last-mile infrastructure are deeply interconnected. I'm interested in what comes after the race for speed — more intelligent distribution, sustainable operations, contextual experiences and better use of scarce resources.

Can we design for the needs of an entire ecosystem, rather than optimise one participant at a time?

06

Human behaviour in a changing world

As technology becomes more embedded in daily life, how do trust, habits, agency and expectations evolve? I want to understand the interplay between human behaviour, technology and policy, and design with that interplay in mind.

As systems become more capable, how do we make sure people become more capable too?

Why I believe I'm equipped to make this leap.

My experience has rarely been confined to one product, one user type or one side of a business. At Swiggy and across my career, I've worked on systems serving millions of consumers as well as the businesses, partners and operational workforces that make those experiences possible.

Where it could go
End-to-end systems thinking

Storefronts, supply chain, inventory, delivery and operational infrastructure — understanding the dependencies behind a consumer promise.

Urban service networks, logistics and mobility ecosystems where the whole system matters as much as its individual parts.

Designing for multiple actors

Experiences for consumers, brands, vendors, suppliers, warehouse teams, store associates and delivery partners, with different needs and incentives.

Platforms spanning institutions, enterprises, service providers and citizens with competing priorities and shared outcomes.

Making the physical and digital work together

Products where software decisions have direct consequences for people, stock, space, time and real-world operations.

Services where technology must understand and accommodate real-world contexts, rather than assume ideal conditions.

Building and scaling

Taking products from 0→1 and 1→N, turning ambiguous problems into coherent experiences and evolving them as complexity grows.

Emerging platforms, digital infrastructure and new service models that need both early experimentation and a path to scale.

Connecting design to outcomes

Balancing business objectives, human needs, operational efficiency and implementation realities across complex product portfolios.

Design decisions informed by public value, accessibility, resilience and long-term ecosystem performance, alongside commercial viability.

Leading across disciplines

Leading designers and partnering with product, engineering, business and operations to translate complexity into direction and execution.

Multidisciplinary leadership across technology, research, policy, infrastructure and service delivery.

The contexts aren't interchangeable. Public systems introduce questions of governance, equity, regulation, privacy and accountability that deserve dedicated learning. I want to bring my experience into those environments with curiosity, rigour and the willingness to learn from people who understand them deeply.

04 / My open questions

Questions I'd love to spend the next chapter answering.

A living research agenda, rather than a set of conclusions. These are the kinds of problems I find myself thinking about long after the workday ends.

01

What if services were organised around moments in people's lives rather than the structures of the organisations delivering them?

02

What does good design look like when the interface is no longer the most important part of the experience?

03

How can intelligence move from individual products into the relationships between systems?

04

How might we anticipate needs without making surveillance or loss of agency the price of convenience?

05

How do we design for an ecosystem to succeed, rather than simply make one participant more efficient?

06

How can we measure the value of design when its most meaningful outcome is time, dignity, trust or a better quality of life?

05 / My aspiration

To help shape systems that are intelligent by design, human by intent.

I want to be at the forefront of defining what comes next — not just designing the experience of a solution, but helping determine which problems are worth solving, how the pieces come together, and what success should mean.

I'm drawn to the space where design leadership, product strategy, emerging technology and real-world infrastructure meet. A place where I can think beyond individual products, work with people from different disciplines, and influence the foundations on which future experiences are built.

I want to retain the joy of making things, the curiosity to question what already exists, and the aspiration to leave the systems I work on better than I found them.

Scale with purpose.Work where the outcomes extend beyond the interface and make a material difference in people's lives.
Think upstream.Help shape the strategy, architecture and principles of a system, not only its final expression.
Keep learning.Work alongside disciplines that challenge what I know and expand what I'm capable of contributing.
