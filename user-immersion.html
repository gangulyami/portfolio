Design leadership / Ways of working

Getting closer to the people behind the product.

User immersion, research rituals & building an organisation that stays close to its users.

Commerce has taken me into very different worlds — from the storefront where a customer makes a choice, to the warehouse where inventory is received, to the business owner investing in growth. The digital interface is only one part of their experience. Understanding the rest of it is a deliberate part of how I design, and how I lead design.

01 / Practice

Immersion as a ritual, not an event.

01 / 04

I have been intentional about creating recurring opportunities to get out of the design file and into the user’s environment. The purpose is not always to answer a specific research question. Sometimes it is to build familiarity with a domain, sharpen our intuition and recognise what is worth investigating next.

An initiative I established

Ground Glimpse

A structured way of staying connected with users, through field immersion and direct conversations. It brings the realities of commerce into the design team's everyday understanding of the products we build.

Ground Glimpse / Field immersion

Field Focus

Site visits to dark stores and warehouses, observing live workflows, walking through the apps people use, and speaking with the people doing the work. Understanding the physical environment is as important as understanding the screen.

Ground Glimpse / User conversations

ServeVoices

Direct engagement with the people our products serve. Conversations bring the user's perspective into discovery, help us interrogate assumptions and provide a space to hear the experience in their own words.

Monthly

Field immersion visits

Instamart B2B Design organises monthly visits, aligned to ongoing projects, research and validation needs.

During discovery

Contextual inquiry & shadowing

Follow real tasks, observe handoffs, ask about workarounds, and understand the tools and people involved beyond the product interface.

Project-led

Interviews & workflow walkthroughs

Use reusable immersion guides to explore users’ responsibilities, goals, current workflows, pain points and decision-making contexts.

Before & after

Validation & feedback

Bring users into the design process before development where possible; revisit the experience after a feature is introduced. Partner Portal research has included both.

Ongoing

Operating forums

In supply-side work, daily operating calls, weekly hygiene discussions and monthly planning reviews offer a window into recurring operational problems and priorities.

One ecosystem. Many human contexts.

A picker working against a live order, a brand manager planning an ad campaign and a customer choosing dinner have very different definitions of a good experience. 03 / Selected observations

What the field brings into focus.

03 / 04

A few examples of the kinds of signals that emerge from looking beyond the interface. The value is in how these observations change the questions we ask as designers.

Dark-store workflows

The screen is not the whole task.

Picking, receiving and inventory counting involve movement through a physical space as well as interactions on a device. Workflow observations and task-time measures help reveal where effort accumulates.

Design lens: Reduce physical and cognitive effort together.
Merchant-facing sales teams

Data work can compete with field work.

Account managers use multiple spreadsheets and data sources to prepare merchant conversations. Data entry can take time away from meeting merchants and growing their businesses.

Design lens: Make information actionable at the point of decision.
Brand & partner conversations

Business context changes the question.

Research across brands of different sizes brings different operating contexts into view. Competitor comparisons and pre- and post-feature validation add perspective beyond isolated feature feedback.

Design lens: Understand the business behind the account.
04 / Leadership

Making user understanding a shared capability.

04 / 04

As a design leader, my role extends beyond participating in research. I want my team to develop the domain fluency, judgment and curiosity that make strong product decisions possible, and for the wider product organisation to understand the people behind those decisions.

Established practice

Make it easier to participate.

I have created reusable research and immersion scripts for designers and stakeholders, organised field engagement and encouraged direct observation as part of the project lifecycle. The intent is to make user access repeatable rather than dependent on an individual initiative.

Operating model / Direction

Bring Product & Engineering closer.

The immersion model I have been working towards brings Design, Product and Engineering into a shared practice. Design facilitates deep user understanding, Product participates in discovery, and Engineering gains context about the environments where its solutions operate.

01 /

Observe

See the work where it happens.

02 /

Understand

Distinguish an isolated issue from a recurring pattern.

03 /

Share

Document evidence and align on implications with the team.

04 /

Revisit

Validate assumptions and return to the real experience.

The intended loop connects field observations, research synthesis, shared documentation and product decisions. Its purpose is to make proximity to users a durable team capability.

The more complex the system, the more important it is to stay close to the people who make it work.
